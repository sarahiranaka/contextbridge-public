# Atlas Customer Research Findings — July 2026

**Document type:** Research Report
**Date:** July 15, 2026
**Owner:** Marcus Lee, User Research
**Audience:** Atlas product and design team

## Background

Following the Q2 review, we expanded the pilot cohort from five design partners to **twenty customers** to get broader signal on the reporting experience ahead of launch. This report summarizes findings from structured interviews and usage analysis conducted between late June and mid-July.

## Finding 1: Static Reports Don't Match How Customers Work

Across nearly every interview, customers described wanting to explore and filter their feedback data directly rather than consume a fixed, point-in-time PDF. Several customers said they currently export competitor reports into spreadsheets just to slice the data themselves. This suggests the planned static PDF reporting experience may not meet customer expectations at launch.

## Finding 2: AI Summary Quality Concerns

Customers who saw early AI summary output in staging were split. Several flagged specific instances where summaries missed important nuance or mischaracterized sentiment. Trust in the summaries was noticeably lower than trust in the underlying tagged feedback data. This is a meaningful risk if summaries ship without further quality work.

## Finding 3: CSV Export Remains a Strong, Recurring Request

Export continues to come up in nearly every conversation, most often framed as "we need to get this data into our own BI tooling." This request has been consistent since our earliest conversations in Q1 and shows no sign of fading.

## Recommendations

- Revisit the static PDF reporting approach before launch
- Invest in AI summary quality validation before relying on summaries as a launch-ready feature
- Continue to treat export as a high-priority, frequently requested capability
